Methods, sources, and licenses

This static viewer presents the existing research snapshot: {{COUNTS}}. It filters and displays recorded results; it does not rescan the corpus, generate new candidates, or recompute scores.

Acrostic candidates

The search checks consecutive encoded Greek verse lines at letter positions 1 and 2, in forward and reverse directions. Candidate strings match normalized word forms occurring at least twice in the corpus, with lengths of 4–12 letters. A highlighted reverse candidate is read from the bottom line upward; passages remain in their original order.

Rarity is the base-2 logarithm of the reciprocal product of the observed letter probabilities at the selected position. Higher values favor longer strings and rarer letters. This is a ranking score, not a probability of deliberate composition. Edition boundaries, line continuity, missing or uncertain readings, corpus selection, and editorial choices affect which candidates are available.

Isopsephy

Only explicitly encoded complete poems or epigrams qualify. The recorded search compares every successive group of one or two lines across the whole poem, requiring at least two groups and exact equality of all group totals. It is not a search for arbitrary equal-sum windows. The viewer shows separate results for omitting or counting subscript iota; an ordinary iota remains a letter in both conventions. Numerical equality alone does not establish intention.

Text and normalization

Original Greek passages are extracted from the pinned TEI editions. The selected readings omit editorial apparatus not used by the parser; textual uncertainty is retained as a warning. The normalized line below each acrostic passage decomposes Greek Unicode, removes accents and breathings, omits combining/subscript iota, folds documented letter variants and final sigma, and uppercases Greek letters. Highlighting uses these stored normalized lines, not the positions of accented Unicode code points. For accented Greek searches the same normalization is applied in the browser. Author, title, and citation searches preserve the local viewer's ASCII case-insensitive matching.

Sources and attribution

Source texts come from the Perseus Digital Library's canonical-greekLit and Open Greek and Latin's First1KGreek. The edition attribution file supplies author, title, CTS URN, source path, pinned source URL, and source-file SHA-256 for each displayed edition. Each selected passage links to its source edition.

Both pinned source repositories supply Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International notices. The extracted and normalized source-text material is distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0. Changes comprise selection/extraction, normalized letter strings, and research annotations. Original notices are included for Perseus and First1KGreek; individual edition headers remain available at the source links.
